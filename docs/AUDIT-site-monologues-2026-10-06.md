# Audit — instruction text nobody reads (wolfpack.quest, 2026-10-06)

The guild lead, 2026-10-06: *"send lower level agents through the entirety of our site and find large instruction
monologues that nobody will read, or if they will it'll be when they have a question."*

How it was done: 11 Haiku agents swept all 96 routes and the shared components (every route group was opened in
full). Each finding was then re-read by a Sonnet agent, which dropped 51 false hits, corrected word counts and
visibility, and wrote a one-line replacement. A final pass ranked them and proposed two site-wide fixes. Nothing has
been changed on the site yet; the fix waits on the guild lead's pick (§168). Line numbers are as of `86a91fc1`.

## 1. TLDR

**130 instruction monologues across 63 routes, 9,945 words.** About **8,900** of those words show on screen by default. The other ~1,050 appear only in empty or conditional states. The three worst pages:
- **/admin**: 825 words. It is a 30-card menu and every card carries a paragraph.
- **/admin/overlays**: 682 words in 9 blocks.
- **/pop**: 533 words in 6 blocks. It is the worst member-facing page; **/raid** (438) and **/roadmap** (427) come next.

The proposed fixes would cut what a reader sees first from ~9,900 words to ~1,650.

- The sweep reported 154 verified findings, but 18 were duplicates: the shared-components sweep found 17 blocks a second time, and the footer was logged three times. That leaves 136 distinct blocks. 6 are KEEP, so 130 need fixing.
- Pages members see: 73 blocks, 5,327 words, 40 routes. Officer pages: 57 blocks, 4,618 words, 23 routes.
- At least 25 of the 130 are out of date, wrong, or developer notes (things like version gates and repo paths). These are deletions, not rewrites.
- The 26 largest blocks (table below) hold 4,069 words, 41% of the total.

## 5. Two ways to fix it site-wide

**A — Fold in place: each explanation stays next to the thing it explains, closed by default.**
**B — One help drawer: a ? in the page header opens all of that page's help in one panel.**

**A — Fold in place.** A reader sees a one-line lead under the page title. Where more is needed, a small "How this works" fold (the browser's built-in `<details>`) or an ⓘ sits on the exact column, badge or switch it explains. The 13 repeats become small shared components (`<Unseen/>`, `<LiveIn/>`, `<NeedsMimic/>`, `<DataSource/>`, `<RaHelp/>`, a privacy line), so each is written once but still shown where it applies.
- **Build:** low per edit, but there are many: about 130 small edits in about 60 files, one thin fold wrapper, and about 6 shared components. It can ship page by page.
- **Maintenance:** low. The help sits in the same file as the control, so anyone changing the feature sees it. The shared components keep the repeats in step.
- **Runtime (mobile first):** lowest. No JavaScript, rendered on the server, and the text stays in the HTML, so crawlers and /ai keep finding it. Pages get shorter, and on a phone the first table moves above the fold.
- **Change:** low. Each fold stands alone, so one can be reworded or reverted without touching the others.

**B — One help drawer.** A reader sees clean pages with a ? in the header. Tapping it opens a side panel (a bottom sheet on phones) with every help section for that page. The text comes from one help file keyed by route, and shared explanations are entries that several pages point to.
- **Build:** medium to high. It needs a drawer component (keyboard focus, Escape to close, scroll lock, bottom sheet on phones), a slot in the header, the help file, and then the same ~130 moves. Each page converts all at once.
- **Maintenance:** medium to high. The help lives in a file that a feature change never opens, so it drifts. /admin/triggers already says "10 min" for a 2-minute poll with the text sitting inline; separating it makes that worse. It would also need a test that every entry still matches a real page.
- **Runtime:** a small script on every page, or an extra request when the drawer opens. On a phone the sheet covers the page, so the reader can't see the column they are asking about while reading the answer.
- **Change:** medium. Rewording is one file, but once pages depend on the drawer, restyling or removing it touches the layout and every entry.

**Recommendation: A.**
- Most of the 130 blocks explain one specific thing: a column, a badge, a switch, or an empty table. A puts the answer where the question comes up; B moves it away.
- At least 25 blocks are out of date or developer notes. Those are deletions, and deletions need no new surface.
- A's shared components already give B's main benefit, writing each explanation once, without B's drift or its runtime cost.
- B is worth revisiting only if pages start piling up long text again after the cut.

**C — One /help page: pages link out to one site-wide help page.** Considered and not recommended. It takes the reader off the page where the question came up. It is the right home only for the few blocks that are really documentation, such as the Raid-Helper pitch (which belongs in the self-host doc) and the /planner formulas.

Under the house rule, these changes are to existing pages, so they go to beta as variants first.
